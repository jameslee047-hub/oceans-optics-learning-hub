## Category: Gear, Masks & Vision

**This category currently teaches:** mask selection at a surface level (lens format, skirt color), with fit/adjustment (R02), preparation/care (R03), snorkel selection (R04), fin selection (R05), and exposure-protection selection (R06) all planned but not yet drafted. Nothing in this category currently teaches prescription-lens mechanics in any depth.

**Obvious missing learner need:** a real answer to "I need vision correction underwater — what do I actually buy?" No existing or planned lesson (R01–R06) explains SPH/CYL, nearsighted vs. farsighted lens strength, or how to translate a glasses prescription into a mask lens choice. This is the gap Part 3/4 below addresses via proposed R31.

### R01 — Choosing a Mask

Category: Gear, Masks & Vision

Purpose: Help a first-time buyer compare the mask decisions that are safe to make before a formal fitting: lens format and skirt color, with vision needs and fit named as considerations.

Planned Coverage (as currently drafted):
- Framing questions to ask before comparing mask styles (vision correction, water brightness/clarity, simplicity vs. specialized needs, fit reminder)
- Single-lens vs. dual-lens masks, including a light touch on dual-lens/prescription relevance
- Clear vs. blackout/dark skirt, by lighting condition
- A five-step practical shortlist ending in "check fit, seal, comfort, and adjustment"

Explicitly Not Covered / Covered Elsewhere:
- Face fit, seal, donning, buckle adjustment → R02
- Defogging, cleaning, aftercare → R03
- Actual prescription mechanics (SPH/CYL/diopter, nearsighted vs. farsighted lens strength) → proposed R31 (not currently covered anywhere)
- Underwater vision/light behavior → R25

Related Lessons: R02, R03, R24, R25

Pathways: Start Here; Equipment Essentials; Future Expansion Pathway "Prescription Masks & Underwater Vision"

Current Status: Partial draft — pilot `public-copy.md` exists and is currently with the external editor. Editorial status is "Pilot draft," publication blocked by product/vision review and instructor input (per `internal-notes.md`). **See Part 5/8 below: the current draft's scope is narrower than its title promises, and structural additions are expected after this audit — treat the current copy pass as a wording check only, not final publication scope.**

Content Gaps: Oceans Optics prescription-mask guidance (see R31 proposal); face-fit/seal expertise (→ R02); comfort/mask-volume guidance (→ R02); lens-selection + underwater-vision expertise (→ R31/R25); intended snorkeling/diving-use guidance.

Review Requirements: product accuracy; prescription vision; instructor input.

## Prescription Masks & Underwater Vision Audit

### What the repository's *educational* content currently covers

The revised source-to-lesson map (`planning/learning-hub-architecture-v2/03-REVISED-SOURCE-TO-LESSON-MAP.md`) accounts for all 129 recovered Genially sources, and **none of them contain prescription-mask mechanics.** The only prescription-adjacent recovered sources are:

- `082 Dual Lens` and `103 Mono Lens` → feed R01, and only support "dual-lens format is connected to prescription-mask support" / "single-lens masks don't support prescription customization" at a surface level.
- `017 Physics` / `113 Light` → feed R24/R25, general underwater light/vision physics, not prescription-specific.

Every planning document that mentions prescription masks in depth (`04-REVISED-LEARNING-PATHWAYS.md`, `05-FUTURE-CONTENT-OPPORTUNITIES.md`, `06-FINAL-HUB-OVERVIEW.md`) explicitly labels it **"FUTURE EXPANSION, not fully supported by recovered Genially material"** and lists the same four contributing lessons: R01, R02, R03, R25. `content-development/CONTENT-DEVELOPMENT-BACKLOG.md` (CG-024 through CG-034) tracks nine prescription-related backlog items, all currently `Parked` / `Future expansion`.

R01's drafted `public-copy.md` currently mentions prescription only twice, both as a *reason to consider* dual-lens format — it does not explain SPH, CYL, diopter, nearsighted vs. farsighted, or lens strength. R02 ("Mask Fit, Positioning & Adjustment") is not yet drafted and would not be expected to cover this either; its scope is physical fit, not prescription selection.

**Conclusion of the education-content search: the premise in this task is correct.** There is currently no lesson, drafted or planned, that teaches prescription-mask mechanics.

### What already exists in the repository's *commercial/tooling* layer

This is the significant finding of this audit: **`theme/learning-hub-pilot/` (the copied live Oceans Optics theme codebase) already contains a real, working prescription-mask business layer** that the Learning Hub content has not yet been connected to. This is not recovered Genially guide material — it is Oceans Optics' actual live commercial infrastructure, so it should be treated as **product-authoritative but not lesson-copy-ready** (it needs the same instructor/fact-check pass as any other source before becoming public lesson wording).

**`sections/lens-calculator.liquid`** (1,152 lines) — the live "Lens Calculator" tool:
- Takes Right/Left eye **SPH** (labeled OD/right, OS/left — standard optometric right/left-eye notation) and **CYL**, with tooltip copy: *"[−] minus for Myopia (nearsighted), [+] plus for Hyperopia (farsighted)"* and *"Check your CYL value and its + or − sign. Select 0.00 if you have no astigmatism."*
- Encodes real per-model diopter ranges/steps (e.g., Rx Rover: −1.50 to −6.00 in 0.50 steps; Rx Obsidian: −1.00 to −9.00; Rx Obsidian Farsighted: +1.00 to +5.00 in 1.00 steps) — **available lens strength varies by mask model**, it is not one universal range.
- Encodes an explicit **unsupported case**: *"We currently do not have masks that support different lens types (nearsighted + farsighted) for each eye. Please contact us for custom solutions."* — mixed-eye (one eye nearsighted, one farsighted) is not an off-the-shelf option today.
- No use of **AXIS** or **ADD** anywhere in the tool — confirms mask lenses are single-vision/spherical only; astigmatism axis and reading-add power are explicitly out of scope for the current product line.

**`sections/fs-cylinder-guide.liquid`** and **`sections/ns-cylinder-guide.liquid`** — "How to account for Cylinder?" guides (farsighted and nearsighted versions) that give a documented, real spherical-equivalent rounding table for converting a full SPH+CYL prescription into a single spherical mask-lens power, by CYL magnitude band (0.00–0.75, 1.00–1.75, 2.00–3.00, 3.25+) and CYL sign. Both link out to a live page, `oceansoptics.com/pages/choosing-prescription-snorkel-mask-lenses-with-astigmatism`, for "detailed examples and explanations" (found as a reference link in the repository; not fetched as part of this audit, since it is outside the repo).

**`snippets/mask_lens_database.liquid`** and **`assets/maskData.js`** — real product data: mask models (Rx Rover, Rx Lumix, Rx Obsidian, Rx Mako, Rx Carbon, Rx Mirage, Rx Action), custom-lens availability, nearsighted diopter steps, and physical sizing dimensions (width/height, cm and inches) by size category (Small/Medium/Large).

**`sections/mask-sizing-tool.liquid`** (602 lines) — the live "Mask Sizing Tool": camera-based or manual-ruler measurement of eye width, mask height, head circumference, and nose size, to recommend a mask by physical fit. **This is a physical-sizing tool, distinct from the Lens Calculator's prescription-strength tool** — R31 should teach learners when to use each.

**`templates/page.nearsighted-or-farsighted.json`** and **`templates/page.which-lens-astigmatism.json`** — existing marketing/education page copy with genuinely useful, already-approved-for-publication-somewhere explanations, including:
- Nearsighted (Myopia): *"you can see nearby objects clearly, but distant objects appear blurry... the eye focuses light in front of the retina... typical prescription -2.50, always negative."*
- Farsighted (Hyperopia): *"you can see distant objects clearly, but close-up objects appear blurry... the eye focuses light behind the retina... common prescription +2.00, always positive."*
- "Sharp Vision" framing for no-correction-needed lenses (Oceans Optics' term for what optometry commonly calls "plano" — the tools do not use the word "plano" itself).
- An underwater-magnification explanation directly relevant to R25: *"objects look about 25% closer... about 33% bigger"* due to the mask's air-pocket refraction, plus mask-lens vertex distance being different from glasses — both reasons a land prescription "might not be your perfect underwater prescription."
- Explicit prescription-reading guidance: *"Use your SPH for each eye. Ignore AXIS. Keep CYL in mind for your decision, but don't try to convert it"* (page copy simplification, not necessarily the same rounding logic as the cylinder guides above — worth reconciling before reuse).

Dozens of related product templates (`product.rx-*`, `product.*-farsighted*`, `product.*-myopia*`), collection templates (`collection.nearsighted-filter.json`, `collection.farsighted-filter.json`, `collection.prescription-masks.json`, `collection.snorkel-prescription-mask.json`, `collection.can-I-wear-contacts-snorkeling.json`), and blog templates (`blog.i-have-an-astigmatism.json`, `blog.mask-sizing.json`) exist in the same theme codebase and were not individually read line-by-line for this audit — they are noted here as further source material to check before drafting R31 in full, not as claims already verified.

### Recommended R31 scope (see Part 4 below for detail)

Because a real product/tooling layer already exists — diopter ranges, a documented SPH+CYL rounding methodology, existing plain-English nearsighted/farsighted copy, and two distinct tools (Lens Calculator, Mask Sizing Tool) — **the "no source material" reason for treating prescription content as future-only no longer fully holds.** See the Backlog Update and Part 7 below for a launch-level reclassification recommendation.

### Recommended R01 scope changes

R01 should keep naming prescription need as a decision factor (as it does today) but should **not** attempt to teach SPH/CYL mechanics itself — that belongs in R31. See Part 5 below for the full current-vs-recommended comparison.

## R31 (Proposed) — Prescription Masks: Nearsighted, Farsighted & Lens Selection

**Status: proposed, not approved. Not created in Shopify. R01–R30 are not renumbered.**

Category: Gear, Masks & Vision

Lesson Purpose: Give a snorkeler who needs vision correction a beginner-safe understanding of what a prescription mask is, how nearsighted/farsighted lens strength is chosen for off-the-shelf mask lenses, and which Oceans Optics tool to use next — without positioning this lesson as a substitute for an eye exam or optometrist advice.

### Learner Objectives

1. Understand what a prescription (corrective) snorkel/dive mask is, at a beginner level.
2. Understand the basic difference between nearsightedness (myopia) and farsightedness (hyperopia) and how each appears on a prescription (negative vs. positive SPH).
3. Identify which parts of a standard eyeglass prescription are relevant to an off-the-shelf corrective mask lens: SPH and CYL are relevant; AXIS and ADD currently are not (per the existing Lens Calculator).
4. Understand at a beginner level what SPH and CYL mean, and that AXIS (astigmatism angle) and ADD (reading power) are not something today's off-the-shelf single-vision mask lenses correct for.
5. Understand what standard off-the-shelf corrective mask lenses can and cannot correct: single-vision spherical correction only; no compound astigmatism correction; lens-strength range and step size vary by mask model.
6. Understand that each eye may need a different lens power, and that today's off-the-shelf product line does not support mixing nearsighted and farsighted correction across the two eyes (custom solutions require contacting Oceans Optics directly).
7. Understand the "plano" / no-correction-needed case (Oceans Optics' existing "Sharp Vision" framing) for a buddy or family member without a prescription need.
8. Understand that mask fit (seal, comfort, size) and prescription-lens compatibility are two separate requirements — a mask can be the right prescription and still not fit, or vice versa.
9. Know when to use the Oceans Optics Lens Calculator (choosing/converting a prescription into a lens-strength selection).
10. Know when to use the Oceans Optics Mask Sizing Tool (choosing a physically well-fitting mask, independent of prescription).
11. Know where deeper lens-selection guidance belongs (link out; this lesson should not try to be the complete astigmatism-conversion guide that `fs-cylinder-guide.liquid` / `ns-cylinder-guide.liquid` already provide elsewhere).

### Planned Headings (draft structure only — not copy)

1. What Is a Prescription Mask?
2. Nearsighted vs. Farsighted, in Plain Terms
3. Reading Your Prescription: What SPH and CYL Mean (and What AXIS/ADD Are, and Why They're Not Used Here)
4. What Off-the-Shelf Mask Lenses Can and Cannot Correct
5. Different Prescriptions in Each Eye (and Today's Mixed-Type Limitation)
6. No Correction Needed? ("Sharp Vision" / Plano Lenses)
7. Fit and Prescription Are Two Separate Decisions
8. Using the Lens Calculator vs. the Mask Sizing Tool
9. Where to Learn More

### Planned Coverage

Beginner-safe explanation of myopia/hyperopia and SPH sign convention; a plain-language description of CYL and why it is approximated rather than exactly corrected in a spherical mask lens (without republishing the full rounding table — link to the existing cylinder guides instead); the mixed-eye/custom-solution caveat; the underwater-magnification note from `which-lens-astigmatism.json` (~25% closer, ~33% bigger) as a reason "your land prescription might not be your perfect underwater prescription," cross-linked to R25; a clear decision hand-off to the two existing tools; and a clear "this is not a substitute for an eye exam" framing.

### Items Requiring Factual Verification

- Confirm current, approved-for-launch diopter ranges/steps per mask model (product data changes; the ranges captured in this audit are a snapshot of `assets/maskData.js` / `lens-calculator.liquid` at audit time).
- Confirm whether the CYL-rounding methodology in `fs-cylinder-guide.liquid`/`ns-cylinder-guide.liquid` is the one Oceans Optics wants referenced in lesson copy, or whether it should be simplified/updated first.
- Confirm the "ignore AXIS" simplification used in `which-lens-astigmatism.json` is still the approved customer guidance (reconcile with the cylinder guides, which discuss CYL sign but not AXIS at all).
- Confirm whether "plano" is the term Oceans Optics wants to introduce to learners, or whether "Sharp Vision" (the existing site term) should be used exclusively.
- Confirm the current custom-lens process for mixed-eye (nearsighted one eye, farsighted the other) prescriptions — the calculator says "contact us," which needs an accurate, current description if referenced in the lesson.
- Optical/vision claims fact-check carries over from backlog CG-006.

### Oceans Optics Instructor/Expert Input Opportunities

- Real customer-facing guidance for the most common prescription mistakes (e.g., using an exact glasses SPH underwater vs. going one step weaker).
- Instructor framing for when a customer should be told to consult their optometrist vs. when the Lens Calculator is sufficient.
- Confirmation of whether reading-add (presbyopia) support is a real customer request Oceans Optics wants flagged as a current gap in the lesson, or omitted entirely.

### Potential Related Products
