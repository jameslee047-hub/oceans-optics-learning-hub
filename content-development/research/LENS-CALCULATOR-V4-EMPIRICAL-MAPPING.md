## Existing Methodology as Understood

Per your clarification, the Oceans Optics empirical methodology is a **banded, discretized adjustment** applied to the sphere-only starting point (SPH), where the size and direction of the adjustment depends on how much astigmatism (CYL) is present — not a spherical-equivalent formula and not a blind minus-cylinder-branch lookup. The grid generated below (see "Old vs V4 Comparison Matrix") confirms the *existing rule set already behaves this way* when read in its native minus-cylinder frame:

- **No/low CYL** → apply the normal underwater reduction (weaker than SPH).
- **Medium CYL** → reduce or remove that reduction (myopia: exactly removed, lands on SPH).
- **High CYL** → for myopia, the reduction stays removed (still lands on SPH); for hyperopia, the rule switches to nearest-whole-number rounding, which can move *stronger* than SPH (see the asymmetry noted below — this is a real, reportable finding, not assumed-away).
- **Very-high CYL** → moves further from SPH in the stronger direction (myopia: +0.75D stronger before snapping; hyperopia: +0.75D stronger before rounding), consistent with drifting toward the spherical-equivalent side, but capped well short of full SE.

This matches your description in structure. What v4 changes is **only** which notation gets fed into this rule set — never the rule set itself.

## Role of SPH

SPH (specifically its magnitude, after normalization) is the anchor value every band's adjustment is computed relative to. It alone determines the "none/low" band output and is the base for every other band's offset.

## Role of CYL Magnitude

CYL magnitude (via `getCylinderBand()`) is the sole selector of *which* adjustment rule applies. It is notation-invariant by construction — `getCylinderBand(|CYL|)` gives the same band whether CYL was originally written as +1.50 or −1.50, since both have magnitude 1.50.

## Role of CYL Sign / SE Direction

This is the crux of the whole refactor. In the **existing, unmodified rule set**, CYL sign is a direct input to some bands (medium, very-high, and the farsighted 0.50/0.75 special case) — but the existing rule set was written assuming CYL sign is *already* expressed in minus-cylinder convention. Post-normalization, CYL sign is always "minus" (or the CYL is exactly zero), so within v4's pipeline, "CYL sign" no longer functions as a customer-facing choice — it functions as a **constant confirming which branch is the tested, minus-cylinder-authored one**. See "Which Existing Branches Become Unreachable" below — this is answered directly and is not glossed over.

## Role of Spherical Equivalent

SE = SPH + CYL/2, computed from either notation, is **provably notation-invariant**: transposing (SPH, CYL, AXIS) → (SPH+CYL, −CYL, AXIS+90) leaves SPH+CYL/2 unchanged, confirmed algebraically and empirically in every row of the generated grid. SE is used in this report **only as a reference direction** — "is the recommendation moving toward or away from SE, and by how much" — never as the recommendation itself. No SE-based formula was implemented or is proposed to replace the existing rule set.

## Role of AXIS

AXIS is collected and used **only** to perform the spherocylindrical transposition (needed to compute normalized SPH/CYL correctly). It plays no role in `calcOne()` — confirmed by inspection: AXIS is not a parameter of `calcOne()` at all, and normalization discards it after producing normalized SPH/CYL. No AXIS-based strength adjustment exists, and none is proposed.

## Available Lens Increment Constraints

Confirmed by construction, not by inspection alone: every nearsighted output passes through `snapToHalf()` or the `-1.00` special case or the `1.00`/`9.00` clamps — all exact multiples of 0.50D. Every farsighted output comes from `Math.floor`/`Math.ceil`/`Math.round` or the `1`/`5` clamps — always a whole number, i.e. exact 1.00D multiples. **The existing algorithm already self-enforces your stated stock increments (0.50D nearsighted, 1.00D farsighted) for every input; this was true before v4 and remains true after.** Clamp range (nearsighted 1.00–9.00, farsighted 1.00–5.00) also matches the product ranges already on record in `LENS-CALCULATOR-SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md`.

**Where empirical target and stock output actually diverge** (from a boundary scan, `raw` = pre-clamp value the formula would produce, `clamped` = what a customer sees):

| SPH | Band | Type | Raw target | Stock output | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.00–1.00 | medium/high/very-high (near) | Nearsighted | −0.50 | **−1.00** | Below minimum stock step; floor-clamped up to the weakest available lens |

## Which Existing Branches Become Unreachable After Normalization?

Answered directly, not assumed: **yes, specific branches become unreachable**, confirmed by code inspection of `calcOne()`'s call site in `calculateEmpiricalRecommendation()`, which always passes `isCylMinus = true` when CYL band is not "none":

1. **Farsighted 0.50/0.75 special case, plus-CYL sub-branch** (`!isCylMinus && cylBand !== "none"` → `+1.00`) — unreachable. Post-normalization this special case always falls to its `else` (`0.00`).
2. **Farsighted "medium" band, plus-CYL sub-branch** (`Math.ceil(val - EPS)`) — unreachable. Always takes `Math.floor(val + EPS)`.
3. **Farsighted "very-high" band, plus-CYL sub-branch** (`Math.round(val + 0.75)`) — unreachable. Always takes `Math.round(Math.max(0, val - 0.75))`.
4. **Nearsighted "medium" band, plus-CYL sub-branch** (`Math.max(0, val - 0.50)`, bias "weaker") — unreachable. Always takes `targetAbs = val`, bias "nearest".
5. **Nearsighted "very-high" band, plus-CYL sub-branch** (`Math.max(0, val - 0.75)`, bias "nearest") — unreachable. Always takes `targetAbs = val + 0.75`.

The "none", "low", and "high" bands (in both refractive directions) never branched on CYL sign in the first place, so they are unaffected — v4 changes nothing about those bands.

This is stated as fact, not assumed correct. Whether it is *desirable* is addressed next.

Reasoning:

1. **For any prescription already written in minus-cylinder notation — the standard, conventional notation most eyeglass prescriptions use — v4's output is byte-identical to the old algorithm's output.** Confirmed across all 78 grid rows. Zero behavior change for the typical case.
2. **The banded rule set's structure (shrinking-then-reversing deduction as CYL band increases) already implements your described conceptual intent (points 1–8) when read in the minus-cylinder frame** — confirmed above in "Existing Methodology as Understood" and the comparison grid.
3. **The "plus-CYL" sub-branches that become unreachable are the ones that would only ever have been exercised by a customer whose prescription happened to be written in plus-cylinder notation, self-selecting the matching sign in the old UI.** If (this is the assumption to confirm) Oceans Optics' underwater testing and iteration was conducted using the conventional minus-cylinder notation — which is by far the more common way prescriptions are written and the more likely basis for real-world testing data — then those plus-branches were **never actually the ones being empirically validated in the first place**. In that case, routing every prescription through the well-tested minus-branch logic is not a departure from tested intent; it is applying the *only genuinely tested* logic consistently, instead of letting an unconverted, untested branch fire for some customers by accident of notation.
4. **The one thing normalization changes for real customers is which physical prescriptions map to which branch** — a prescription physically equivalent to a tested minus-cylinder case, but previously misrouted into an untested plus-branch because of how it was written, now correctly receives the tested minus-branch answer. That is the fix, and it is consistent with — not a departure from — empirical intent, *provided assumption #3 holds*.

**What would change this answer to PARTIALLY or NO:** if Oceans Optics can confirm that real underwater testing specifically included plus-cylinder-notation entries and validated those "now-unreachable" branches as intentionally different (not just mathematically symmetric guesses), then removing access to those branches would be a genuine, if narrow, loss of tested behavior for that specific customer population, and would need to be revisited.

## Candidate Notation-Independent Model

Described conceptually only — **not coded, not to be coded this task**:

The v4 pipeline (normalize to minus-cylinder → classify severity band by CYL magnitude → apply the existing per-band rule, unchanged) **already is** a notation-independent expression of your description: SE is available as a directional reference (and is reported per-row above), CYL magnitude alone selects severity, and the existing empirically-tuned adjustment is applied without alteration. No further model change is needed to satisfy the stated goal.

A cleaner *future* formalization — not proposed for implementation now — would express each band's adjustment as an explicit function of "fractional distance from SPH toward SE" rather than a hand-tuned fixed offset per band (e.g., 0.75D flat offset for very-high, regardless of whether the actual CYL is 3.25 or 8.00). That would be a genuine redesign of the empirical formula and is explicitly out of scope; it is noted only as a possible long-term research direction, not a recommendation to pursue now.

## Cases Where Recommendations Would Change

Only prescriptions that were previously **entered by a customer in plus-cylinder notation with the matching plus sign selected** would see a different recommendation under v4 versus the old tool, for the same physical eye. Any customer who already entered minus-cylinder notation sees no change. The "Equivalent Prescription Analysis" table above shows the magnitude of the change for 8 representative cases (up to 2.50D swing in the tested examples). This is the intended correction, not a defect.

## Risks

1. **Hyperopic "high"-band asymmetry** (flagged above): moves stronger and away from SE, unlike every other band. Existing, unmodified behavior — but worth an explicit confirmation that this was empirically intended and not, e.g., an accidental `Math.round` vs `Math.floor` choice that was never distinguished from the myopic side during testing.
2. **Assumption #3 above (testing notation) is unconfirmed.** This is the single largest risk to the "YES" conclusion and should be confirmed with James/Oceans Optics before treating v4's behavior-shift-for-plus-notation-entries as fully validated.
3. **Non-0.25-step CYL values** (e.g., a prescription genuinely written with a non-standard CYL) are currently rejected by v4's validation; real-world prescriptions are overwhelmingly on 0.25 steps, but this should be confirmed as an acceptable constraint.
4. **AXIS collection adds friction** to the input flow for a value that, once used for transposition, has no further effect — worth confirming the UX cost is acceptable for the correctness gained.
