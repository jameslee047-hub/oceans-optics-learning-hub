## Lesson Purpose
Help a first-time buyer make the mask decisions that are safe to make before fitting or prescription depth — lens format, skirt color, and intended use — while clearly naming fit and prescription need as separate, linked decisions this lesson does not itself teach. **The lesson should teach someone how to make a good mask-selection decision regardless of what brand they ultimately buy** — see Product-Neutral Policy below.

## Product-Neutral Educational Policy (resolves CG-062)

**Decision:** the educational lesson body stays product-neutral. Oceans Optics products, collections, the Lens Calculator, the Mask Sizing Tool, and any "Find Your Mask" tools may appear only as clearly separated **related tools**, **next steps**, **optional product callouts**, or **commercial CTA blocks** — never woven into the core explanation of how to choose a mask. No section's teaching should depend on buying an Oceans Optics product to make sense. This applies to every section below; it is not repeated in each one.

This resolves the product-neutral-vs-product-linked policy question. It does **not** resolve whether snorkeling-vs-scuba use materially changes mask-selection advice — that remains a separate, open instructor question (see Section 1).

## Learner Outcomes
After this lesson the learner should be able to / understand:
- What to consider before comparing mask styles (intended use, vision need, conditions)
- The difference between single-lens and dual-lens masks, and that lens layout can matter for prescription users specifically
- What mask volume means, at a basic planning level
- The difference between clear and dark/black skirts, and what each actually changes (peripheral light — not water clarity)
- That fit/seal and prescription selection are real, separate next steps — and where to go for each
- A practical decision sequence for narrowing down a mask
- That the Mask Sizing Tool exists as an optional next step for narrowing physical model/size by face measurements

### 5. Single-Lens vs. Dual-Lens Masks

Purpose: Compare the two lens formats, with prescription relevance stated accurately rather than absolutely.

Teaching points:

**Single-lens mask:**
- One continuous viewing lens
- Within the standard interchangeable/off-the-shelf prescription-mask system, single-lens masks do not support separate interchangeable corrective lenses for the left and right eyes
- Do not state or imply that no custom single-lens prescription solution could ever exist anywhere — that is a broader claim than this lesson can support

**Dual-lens mask:**
- Separate left and right lenses
- Can support interchangeable prescription lenses, depending on the specific mask model
- May allow a different supported lens power in each eye
- Not every dual-lens mask is automatically prescription-compatible

**Core learner message (use this framing):** *"If you need prescription correction, dual-lens masks are commonly used for interchangeable corrective lenses, but prescription compatibility still depends on the specific mask model."*

Keep this section genuinely useful even for a learner who does not need prescription correction — it's a mask-selection factor in its own right, not only a prescription topic.

**Do not introduce, without independent verification:**
- Dual-lens gives better clarity
- Dual-lens gives a wider field of view
- Single-lens gives better field of view
- One format inherently has less distortion
- One format is universally better

### 6. Clear vs. Dark/Black Skirt

Purpose: Compare skirt color by what it actually changes — peripheral light, not water clarity.

Teaching points:

**Clear skirt:**
- Allows more ambient/peripheral light into the mask
- Can create a brighter, more open visual feeling
- Some users prefer this less-enclosed feeling

**Dark/black skirt:**
- Blocks more peripheral light
- Can reduce distracting peripheral light, reflections, or glare
- May give a more focused visual experience
- May be preferred in bright conditions

**Approved core fact (internally fact-checked and approved 2026-08-28, no further verification needed):** clear silicone skirts allow more ambient/peripheral light into the mask; dark/black skirts block more peripheral light. Use this as settled planning information.

**Important — remove and do not reintroduce:** any wording implying "clear skirts improve visibility in murky water," or that skirt color affects how clear suspended particles or low-visibility water appear. Murky-water visibility and the amount of ambient/peripheral light entering around the mask skirt are separate concepts. Do not conflate them.

- Skirt color affects peripheral light, not water clarity
- Fit/seal matters most, but is taught in R02
- Prescription need is a real, separate decision — see R31
- A short, repeatable decision sequence beats comparing every feature at once
