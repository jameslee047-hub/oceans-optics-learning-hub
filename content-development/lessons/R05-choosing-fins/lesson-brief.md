# R05 — Choosing Fins

## Curriculum Status
Category: Gear, Masks & Vision
Pathways: Equipment Essentials
Curriculum decision: KEEP
Related lessons: R21, R22, R06

## Lesson Purpose
Help learners compare fin formats (open-heel vs. closed-heel, long vs. short) and understand basic fin aftercare.

## Learner Outcomes
After this lesson the learner should be able to / understand:
- Compare open-heel and closed-heel fins
- Compare long and short fins
- Understand basic fin aftercare

## Scope Boundary

### This lesson teaches
- Open-heel vs. closed-heel comparison
- Long vs. short fin comparison
- Aftercare

### This lesson introduces but does not teach in depth
- Kicking technique/efficiency implications (e.g., "long fins suit powerful straight-line kicking") → detailed home: R21, R22

### This lesson does NOT cover
- How to kick efficiently → R21, R22
- Exposure protection/boots as a separate gear decision → R06 (only mention that open-heel fins may need boots)

## Proposed Teaching Sequence

### 1. Open-Heel vs. Closed-Heel Fins

Purpose: Compare the two heel styles.

Teaching points:
- Open-heel: more flexible/adjustable for various conditions; typically needs dive boots for secure fit and abrasion protection
- Closed-heel: ideal for warm water, no boots needed, lightweight/compact for travel; leaves feet exposed (not ideal for cold water)

### 2. Long vs. Short Fins

Purpose: Compare fin length tradeoffs.

Teaching points:
- Long fins: more propulsion/power, better for deep/open water and current, need stronger legs, less agile, not travel-friendly, popular with freedivers/spearfishers
- Short fins: more comfortable, better for shallow water (avoids seabed contact/damage), beginner-friendly, more agile, travel-friendly, useful for underwater photography

Source basis:
- LEGACY OCEANS OPTICS SOURCE (`extracted-content/pages/064-long-fins.md`, `073-short-fins.md`)

Instructor/expert input:

Visual opportunity:

### 3. Aftercare

Purpose: Basic post-use care.

Teaching points:
- Rinse with fresh water; scrub gently if needed
- Dry completely before storing; avoid direct sunlight
- Mold prevention: vinegar soak if needed

Source basis:
- LEGACY OCEANS OPTICS SOURCE (`extracted-content/pages/042-after-care.md`)

## Key Takeaways
- Heel style affects footwear needs and water-temperature suitability
- Fin length trades power/speed against agility and travel convenience
- Short fins reduce the risk of seabed/reef contact
- Same rinse/dry/mold-prevention routine as other gear

## Common Mistakes Opportunities
- Choosing long fins without the leg strength/conditioning to use them comfortably
- Choosing closed-heel fins for cold water and being caught without foot protection
- Using long fins in shallow reef areas, risking seabed/coral contact

## Knowledge Check Plan

What understanding should be tested:
- Matching fin type to its main tradeoff
- Understanding that fin choice affects more than comfort (e.g., reef-contact risk, fatigue)

Proposed question concepts:
1. Which fin style typically requires dive boots? — catches confusion between open- and closed-heel.
2. Why might short fins be a better choice for shallow reef snorkeling? — catches the misconception that longer/more powerful fins are always better.
3. What is a drawback of long fins for a beginner? — catches the misconception that more propulsion is always an advantage.
