## Why You Get Cold in Water

Water pulls heat away from your body far more efficiently than air does, which is why you can feel chilled snorkeling in water that would feel comfortable as air temperature. Understanding why helps explain why exposure gear matters even in water that doesn't feel especially cold at first.

## Why Water Cools You Faster Than Air

Water conducts heat away from your body much more efficiently than air. That means your body loses warmth faster in water than it would sitting in air at the same temperature, even water that feels mild on first entry can gradually draw down your body temperature over time. This is why a longer snorkel in cool water can leave you feeling cold even if the first few minutes felt fine.

## Recognizing the Early Signs

Feeling a bit cool at first is normal, but shivering, becoming clumsy, or feeling unusually tired are signs your body is working to stay warm and it's time to get out and warm up. Catching this early is easier and more comfortable than waiting until you're seriously chilled.

## Overheating Is Also Possible

In warm water, especially with a wetsuit on, the opposite problem can occur: overheating. Staying hydrated and taking breaks in the shade between snorkels helps you avoid heat-related discomfort, particularly in tropical conditions.

Understanding why you get cold is the reasoning behind choosing the right exposure protection for the conditions you'll be in. ## Safety Note

If you or a buddy start shivering uncontrollably, feel unusually clumsy or confused, or feel excessively tired while in the water, treat it as a sign to exit and warm up. In warm conditions, watch for signs of overheating like dizziness or nausea, and take breaks in shade with water to drink.

## Instructor Tips

- Use a relatable comparison, like how a swimming pool that feels fine at first can leave you shivering an hour later, to make heat loss concrete.
- Mention that even tropical water can eventually feel cold on a long snorkel, since heat loss is gradual and cumulative.
- Don't skip the overheating side, it's a real risk in a wetsuit under a hot sun and often overlooked.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming water that feels fine at first will stay comfortable for the whole outing.
- Ignoring early signs like shivering or clumsiness instead of exiting to warm up.
- Overlooking the risk of overheating while wearing a wetsuit in warm, sunny conditions.

## Key Takeaways

- Water draws heat from your body much more efficiently than air.
- Feeling fine at first doesn't mean you won't get cold over a longer snorkel.
- Shivering, clumsiness, or unusual tiredness are signs to exit and warm up.
- Overheating is also possible in warm water, especially while wearing a wetsuit.
