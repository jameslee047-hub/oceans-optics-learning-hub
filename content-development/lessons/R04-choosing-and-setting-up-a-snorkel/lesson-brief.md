# R04 — Choosing and Setting Up a Snorkel

## Curriculum Status
Category: Gear, Masks & Vision
Pathways: Equipment Essentials
Curriculum decision: KEEP
Related lessons: R18, R19, R15

## Lesson Purpose
Compare snorkel types and explain attachment, placement, and aftercare.

## Learner Outcomes
After this lesson the learner should be able to / understand:
- Compare dry, traditional J-shape, and flexible snorkels
- Attach a snorkel to the mask strap
- Position a snorkel correctly on the head
- Perform basic snorkel aftercare

## Proposed Teaching Sequence

### 1. Snorkel Types: Dry, J-Shape, and Flexible

Purpose: Compare the three recovered snorkel types.

Teaching points:
- Dry snorkel: float/cover prevents water ingress, purge-style easy clearing; bulkier and more expensive
- Traditional J-shape: simple, affordable, beginner-friendly; requires forceful exhalation to clear
- Flexible snorkel: adjustable mouthpiece reduces jaw fatigue; less direct airflow, harder to clear than purge style

### 2. Attaching the Snorkel

Purpose: Teach basic attachment to the mask strap.

Teaching points:
- Locate the snorkel keeper/clip on the mask strap (typically at the back)
- Slide the snorkel through until secure

Source basis:
- LEGACY OCEANS OPTICS SOURCE (`extracted-content/pages/044-attatching-the-snorkel.md`)

Instructor/expert input:
- None required.

Visual opportunity:
- Photo of the keeper/clip in use — OPTIONAL.

### 3. Snorkel Placement

Purpose: Teach correct head position and angle.

Teaching points:
- Position on the side aligned with the dominant hand
- Angle so it slopes upward toward the surface
- Keep the tip just above the water surface when floating face-down; avoid fully submerging it
- Maintain a relaxed, neutral head position (face parallel to the water surface)

Source basis:
- LEGACY OCEANS OPTICS SOURCE (`extracted-content/pages/036-snorkel-placement.md`)

Instructor/expert input:
- Confirm "dominant hand side" is still the guidance taught — a specific, checkable convention worth a quick sanity check.

Visual opportunity:
- Annotated photo of correct placement/angle — ESSENTIAL.

### 4. Aftercare

Purpose: Basic post-use care.

Teaching points:
- Rinse with fresh water; scrub gently if needed
- Dry completely before storing; avoid direct sunlight
- Mold prevention: dry fully, vinegar soak if mold appears

## Key Takeaways
- Snorkel type affects clearing effort and comfort, not just looks
- Dry snorkels trade bulk/cost for easier clearing
- Correct placement (side, angle, head position) affects comfort and dry breathing
- Same rinse/dry/mold-prevention routine as other gear

## Instructor Tips Opportunities
- Real guidance on which snorkel type Oceans Optics recommends for beginners vs. experienced snorkelers — not yet supplied, do not invent

## Common Mistakes Opportunities
- Choosing a J-shape snorkel without realizing it requires forceful exhalation to clear
- Submerging the snorkel tip fully instead of keeping it just above the surface
- Tilting the head too far forward or backward, letting water in

## Safety Notes
NONE. Equipment-selection lesson. If a claim implies "dry snorkels are safer," treat that as a claim needing fact-check, not an established safety fact — dry snorkels reduce water ingress but are not a safety device.

## Knowledge Check Plan

What understanding should be tested:
- Matching snorkel type to its main tradeoff, not just its name
- Correct snorkel angle/placement concept

Proposed question concepts:
1. Which snorkel type requires forceful exhalation to clear water? — catches confusion between the three types' clearing methods.
2. Where should the snorkel tip be positioned relative to the water surface while floating? — catches the misconception that fully submerging the tip is fine.
3. What should you check first if you're having trouble breathing through your snorkel? — catches head-position/angle as a first troubleshooting step, not assuming faulty equipment.
