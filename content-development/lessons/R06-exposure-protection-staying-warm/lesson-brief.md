# R06 — Exposure Protection & Staying Warm

## Curriculum Status
Category: Gear, Masks & Vision
Pathways: Equipment Essentials (R01 → R31 → R02 → R03 → R04 → R05 → R06 → R27 → R09)
Curriculum decision: KEEP
Related lessons: R10, R27

## Lesson Purpose
Explain exposure protection (sunscreen, rash guard, wetsuit) as an equipment-selection and preparation decision, cross-linked to R27's heat-transfer physiology.

## Learner Outcomes
After this lesson the learner should be able to / understand:
- Basic sun-protection habits for snorkeling
- What a rash guard does and why it's commonly recommended
- Wetsuit basics: how neoprene works, fit, aftercare
- That this lesson explains "what to buy/use"; R27 explains "why it works"

## Scope Boundary

### This lesson teaches

### This lesson does NOT cover

## Proposed Teaching Sequence

### 1. Sun Protection

Purpose: Cover basic sun-safety habits specific to snorkeling.

Teaching points:
- Apply reef-safe sunscreen roughly 30 minutes before sun/water exposure
- A rash guard is a strong option for snorkeling; cover the back to avoid burns
- Water intensifies sun exposure, so burns can appear faster/more severe

### 2. Wetsuit Basics

Purpose: Explain how a wetsuit keeps you warm and how to choose thickness.

Teaching points:
- Neoprene traps a thin layer of water that your body heats, acting as insulation
- Thickness (mm) is chosen based on water temperature; thicker means warmer but less flexible

## Key Takeaways
- Reef-safe sunscreen and a rash guard are the basic sun-protection combo for snorkeling
- Wetsuit thickness is a warmth-vs-flexibility tradeoff
- A good wetsuit fit is snug but not restrictive
- Basic aftercare (rinse, shade-dry, check for tears) extends wetsuit life

## Common Mistakes Opportunities
- Applying sunscreen right before entering the water instead of ~30 minutes ahead
- Skipping back coverage on a rash guard/sunscreen
- Choosing wetsuit thickness based on air temperature instead of water temperature
- Drying a wetsuit in direct sunlight

## Safety Notes
NONE for equipment selection itself. **The sunburn-severity claim ("burns appear faster, more severe" in water) borders on a health claim — recommend a fact-check pass rather than a full safety review, since it is informational, not emergency-response instruction.**

## Knowledge Check Plan

What understanding should be tested:
- Why water exposure changes sun-protection needs
- The neoprene warmth mechanism (traps and heats a water layer, not "keeps water out")
- Matching wetsuit thickness choice to water temperature, not air temperature

Proposed question concepts:
1. Why might sunburn happen faster while snorkeling than on land? — catches the misconception that being in water reduces sun-exposure risk.
2. How does a wetsuit actually keep you warm? — catches the common misconception that a wetsuit keeps water out entirely.
3. What should guide your choice of wetsuit thickness? — catches the misconception that air temperature, rather than water temperature, is the right reference point.
