## Lesson Purpose
Give learners a practical, sequential fitting flow — donning, positioning, adjusting, and comfort-checking a mask — for a mask they have already selected.

## Learner Outcomes
After this lesson the learner should be able to / understand:
- Don a mask correctly
- Position the strap on the crown of the head, above the ears
- Adjust the buckle/strap evenly on both sides
- Run a comfort check before entering the water
- Handle long hair and facial hair as fit considerations
- Understand that a Mask Sizing Tool recommendation (from R01) is a starting point, not a substitute for the physical fit check this lesson teaches

## Scope Boundary

### This lesson teaches
- Donning technique
- Strap/buckle positioning and adjustment
- Comfort check
- Long hair / facial hair accommodation

### This lesson introduces but does not teach in depth
- Face-size/model selection → tool cross-link only (Mask Sizing Tool); the actual model/size decision belongs earlier in the buyer journey, in R01, not here
- The Mask Sizing Tool's recommendation → R02 explains how it *relates to* actual fit, without re-teaching model selection (see Section 7)

### This lesson does NOT cover

- Defogging, cleaning → R03

- Prescription-specific lens selection → R31

## Face Sizing vs. Seal/Comfort Fitting vs. Strap Adjustment — Scope Check (RESOLVED 2026-08-28)

This was the open question this brief originally flagged. It is now resolved:

- **Face sizing / model selection** (choosing which mask model and size fits your face shape, by eye width, mask height, head circumference, and nose size) is what the **Mask Sizing Tool** does. It is a "which product should I buy" decision, introduced in **R01** as an optional next step.
- **Seal/comfort fitting** (whether the mask you already have seals correctly and feels comfortable) and **strap positioning/adjustment** (how you physically wear the mask you already have) are what **this lesson (R02)** teaches. It is a "how do I use what I bought, and does it actually work" decision.

**Resolved placement: the Mask Sizing Tool is used in both R01 and R02, with different roles.** R01 introduces it as a pre-purchase, model/size-narrowing step. R02 (Section 7 below) explains that the tool's recommendation *relates to, but does not replace,* the physical seal test, comfort check, and strap positioning this lesson teaches — a tool recommendation is a starting point, not a guarantee of fit. R02 is **not** expanded into a face-sizing lesson; it stays focused on technique for a mask already in hand.

### 7. What the Mask Sizing Tool Does and Doesn't Replace

Purpose: Explain how a Mask Sizing Tool recommendation (from R01) relates to the physical fit check this lesson teaches — supplementing, not replacing, actual fit assessment. This section does not re-teach model selection.

Teaching points:
- A Mask Sizing Tool recommendation is a starting point based on face measurements — it is not a guarantee that the mask will seal and feel comfortable on your specific face
- The physical seal test, comfort check, and strap positioning taught in this lesson (Sections 1–6) are still required after using the tool, not optional extras
- If discomfort persists even after correct donning/adjustment, the issue may be model/size, not technique — in that case, revisit the Mask Sizing Tool (introduced in R01) rather than assuming the fitting technique is at fault

## Key Takeaways
- Loosen straps before donning
- Seal placement and strap position both affect fit
- Adjust both sides evenly
- The 1–2 cm comfort check is a simple, repeatable test
- Persistent discomfort after correct technique may mean the wrong model/size, not a technique problem

## Common Mistakes Opportunities
- Donning with straps still tight, causing discomfort or a poor seal
- Uneven strap adjustment (one side tighter than the other)
- Assuming persistent leaks are a technique problem when they may be a size/model mismatch
- Positioning the strap too low (below the crown) rather than above the ears

## Safety Notes
NONE. This is a technique/comfort lesson, not a safety-critical one. If "why fit matters" is ever framed around drowning/water-entry risk, treat that as a cross-link to R07/R08, not new safety content here.

## Knowledge Check Plan

What understanding should be tested:
- Correct strap position (crown/above ears, not too low)
- The comfort-check concept (small gap, no pressure) vs. "as tight as possible"
- Recognizing when a fit problem is likely model/size rather than technique

Proposed question concepts:
1. Where should the mask strap rest? — catches the misconception that tighter/lower is always better.
2. What does a correct comfort check feel like? — catches the misconception that a mask should feel maximally tight to seal well.
3. If a mask still leaks after correct donning and adjustment, what should you check next? — catches the misconception that all fit problems are technique problems.
