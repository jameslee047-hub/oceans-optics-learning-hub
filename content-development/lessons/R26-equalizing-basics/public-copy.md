## Equalizing Basics

As you descend even a short distance below the surface, pressure builds on the airspaces in your ears and sinuses. Equalizing relieves that pressure so you stay comfortable and avoid injury. See [Buoyancy, Pressure & Airspaces](lesson:buoyancy-pressure-and-airspaces) for the science behind why this happens.

## Why Equalizing Matters

Without equalizing, increasing pressure on your middle ear and sinuses can cause discomfort, pain, and in more serious cases injury known as barotrauma. The fix is straightforward: adjust the pressure in these airspaces to match the surrounding water pressure as you go down.

## When to Start

Begin equalizing early, at the first sign of pressure, rather than waiting until it's uncomfortable. Even shallow depths of half a meter or so can be enough to notice the effect. Equalizing little and often as you descend is more comfortable than trying to catch up after the fact.

## Common Equalizing Methods

**Valsalva maneuver:** Pinch your nose closed, close your mouth, and gently blow out through your nose. This helps open the Eustachian tube and relieve pressure in the middle ear.

**Frenzel maneuver:** Pinch your nose as with the Valsalva method, but instead of blowing forcefully, make a "K" sound while trying to push air through your nose. Many people find this a more controlled alternative.

**Jaw wiggle:** With your mouth closed, move your jaw side to side and back and forth. This movement alone can help open the Eustachian tube.

**Yawn method:** Simulate a yawn by opening your mouth wide and stretching your jaw. Like the jaw wiggle, this can help open the Eustachian tube without needing to pinch your nose.

## Tips for Comfortable Equalizing

- Never force it. If a method isn't working, forcing it can cause discomfort or injury.
- You should feel gentle relief in both ears after equalizing, not strain or pain.
- If you're having difficulty, ascend slightly and try again rather than pushing further down.
- Clearing your nose before descending can make equalizing easier by keeping your nasal passages clear.

## Safety Note

Never force an equalizing attempt. If you feel pain, resistance, or difficulty equalizing, stop and ascend slightly rather than continuing to descend. Equalizing should bring gentle relief, not strain. If you have a cold, sinus congestion, or any condition affecting your ears or sinuses, consult a medical professional before diving, since equalizing can be more difficult or risky under those conditions.

## Instructor Tips

- Introduce equalizing as a "little and often" habit rather than something to do only when it starts to hurt.
- Let learners try each method at the surface first so they know the motion before combining it with a descent.
- Reinforce "if it's not working, go back up a bit" as the default response to difficulty, not pushing through.

## Common Mistakes

- Waiting until pressure is painful before starting to equalize.
- Forcing an equalizing attempt instead of easing off and trying again.
- Continuing to descend despite difficulty equalizing.
- Diving with a cold or sinus congestion without medical guidance.

## Key Takeaways

- Equalizing relieves pressure building in your ears and sinuses as you descend.
- Start equalizing early and repeat it often, not just when it becomes uncomfortable.
- Valsalva, Frenzel, jaw wiggle, and yawn are four common equalizing methods.
- Never force equalizing, if it's not working, ascend slightly and try again.
