## Clearing Your Snorkel

Water entering your snorkel is a normal part of snorkeling, whether from a wave, submerging briefly, or just breathing technique. Clearing it quickly and calmly is a basic, essential skill. For background on snorkel designs and features like purge valves, see [Choosing and Setting Up a Snorkel](lesson:choosing-and-setting-up-a-snorkel).

## The Blast Method

When water enters your snorkel, exhale forcefully and sharply through your mouth to push the water out through the top of the tube. This is often called the blast method, and it works on any snorkel, regardless of design.

The key is a sharp, forceful exhale, not a slow one. A gentle breath out will not generate enough force to clear the water.

Water doesn't always fully clear on the first try. Breathe back in cautiously rather than assuming the tube is clear, and blast again if you feel any water remaining.

## How a Purge Valve Helps

Many modern snorkels include a purge valve near the mouthpiece, a one-way valve that lets water drain out the bottom of the tube instead of requiring you to blast it all the way out the top. If your snorkel has a purge valve, clearing water usually takes less effort and less force than the full blast method.

Even with a purge valve, a firm exhale is still often needed to fully clear the tube. The valve reduces the effort required, it does not eliminate the need to clear at all.

## Staying Calm When Water Enters

Water entering your snorkel can feel startling the first few times it happens, but it is a normal, expected part of snorkeling. Stop, exhale forcefully to clear the tube, and resume normal breathing once it's clear. If you're ever unable to clear it or feel panicked, lift your head above the surface and breathe normally until you're ready to continue.

## Instructor Tips

- Practice clearing your snorkel in shallow, calm water before you need to rely on it in tougher conditions, the technique should already feel automatic before it actually matters.

## Common Mistakes

- Exhaling too gently to generate enough force to clear the tube.
- Panicking when water enters instead of simply clearing and continuing.
- Assuming a purge valve means no exhale effort is ever needed.

## Key Takeaways

- Clear a flooded snorkel with a sharp, forceful exhale through the mouth.
- Water doesn't always fully clear on the first try, breathe back in cautiously and blast again if needed.
- A purge valve reduces effort but doesn't eliminate the need to clear.
- Water entering the snorkel is normal, not a sign something is wrong.
- If you can't clear it or feel panicked, surface and breathe normally first.

## Knowledge Check

Q1. What is the "blast method" for clearing a snorkel?

- A. Removing the snorkel from your mouth entirely
- B. Inhaling deeply until the water clears itself
- C. A sharp, forceful exhale through the mouth to push water out

Correct: C

Explanation: A forceful, sharp exhale generates enough pressure to push water out through the top of the tube.

Q2. How does a purge valve change the clearing process?

- A. It lets water drain from the bottom, usually reducing the effort needed
- B. It removes the need to exhale entirely
- C. It has no effect on clearing

Correct: A

Explanation: A purge valve gives water an exit point near the mouthpiece, which usually means less force is needed compared to a full blast to the top.

Q3. What should you do if you feel panicked after water enters your snorkel?

- A. Keep swimming and ignore it
- B. Surface, lift your head above water, and breathe normally before continuing
- C. Remove your mask immediately

Correct: B

Explanation: Surfacing to breathe normally is a simple way to reset if clearing the snorkel feels stressful in the moment.

Q4. Why is a gentle exhale usually not enough to clear a snorkel?

- A. Gentle exhales work better than forceful ones
- B. The exhale strength doesn't matter at all
- C. It doesn't generate enough force to push the water out

Correct: C

Explanation: Clearing water requires enough air pressure to push it out of the tube, which a slow, gentle breath typically cannot generate.
