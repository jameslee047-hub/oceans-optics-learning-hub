## Relaxed Breathing & Efficient Movement

A calmer, more efficient snorkel comes from two simple habits: relaxed breathing and a streamlined body position. Neither takes special fitness, just a bit of practice and attention.

## Relaxed Breathing at the Surface

Breathe slowly and evenly through your snorkel rather than in short, rushed breaths. Rushed breathing tends to feed anxiety and burns more energy than it needs to. If you notice your breathing speeding up, that's a good cue to slow down, relax your shoulders, and let your breathing settle before continuing.

## Efficient Movement

Let your fins do the work. Keep your arms relaxed at your sides or gently in front of you rather than paddling with them, most of your propulsion should come from your legs and fins, not your arms. Keep your body as flat and streamlined as possible at the surface, this reduces drag and makes each kick more effective.

Small, controlled movements generally cover more distance for less effort than large, forceful ones. If you find yourself getting tired quickly, it's often a sign to slow down and streamline rather than to kick harder.

## Why This Matters Beyond Comfort

Relaxed breathing and efficient movement aren't just about comfort, they connect directly to two other skills. Staying calm and unhurried is part of the foundation for [Breath-Hold Safety, CO2 & Shallow-Water Blackout](lesson:breath-hold-safety-co2-and-shallow-water-blackout), and efficient movement at the surface carries over directly into [Duck Diving Basics](lesson:duck-diving-basics), where controlled, unhurried motion matters even more.

## Instructor Tips

- Use "slow it down" as a simple correction for both rushed breathing and over-kicking, they often show up together.
- Have learners practice floating motionless and breathing calmly before adding movement.
- Point out that tiredness is often a sign to streamline, not to kick harder.

## Common Mistakes

- Paddling with the arms instead of letting the fins provide propulsion.
- Breathing quickly and shallowly instead of slow and even.
- Kicking harder when tired, instead of slowing down and relaxing the body position.

## Key Takeaways

- Slow, even breathing reduces both anxiety and energy use while snorkeling.
- Most propulsion should come from your fins, not your arms.
- A flat, streamlined body position reduces drag and improves efficiency.
- Getting tired quickly is usually a cue to slow down, not kick harder.
